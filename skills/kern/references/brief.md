# The brief

Before designing anything new, ask the person what they want. They often already have a picture in their head: a site they love, a feeling, a thing they never want to see again. A design that is distinctive but is not what they pictured has failed. Kern's taste is there to fill gaps and to raise the craft, not to overrule the person it is working for.

The brief is a short conversation, not a form. One round, a few questions, then work.

## When to ask

- **Ask** at the start of any new design, redesign or big restyle: a new site, app, dashboard, deck or brand surface.
- **Skip** on narrow fixes, follow-ups inside a design you already agreed, and anything where the person said "just build it", "surprise me" or "you decide". Then state your assumptions in one line each and proceed.
- **Skip what is already answered.** Read the request, the repository, any existing brand, the existing site and `.kern/record.md` first. Never ask something you could have found out.
- **Ask once.** Gather everything in one round. Later questions are only for facts that genuinely block the work.

## How to ask

- **Use the platform's question tool** when there is one (multiple-choice prompts in the agent's interface). Otherwise ask in one short message with numbered questions.
- **Three to five questions,** never more than six. Pick the ones whose answers would most change the design.
- **Write the options from this brief,** not from a fixed catalogue. Options for a ferry company and for a synth maker should share nothing. Two to four concrete options each, phrased as outcomes the person can picture ("Feels like the printed timetable at the pier", not "Minimal").
- **Always allow "you decide".** Put your recommendation first and say it is your pick.
- **Make it fast to answer.** Short questions, short options, no design jargon. Someone should finish in under a minute.

## The questions

Choose from these. Rewrite each in the person's own terms.

1. **The picture in their head.** "Do you already imagine how it should look or feel?" Offer two or three directions derived from the ground (each named after something real in their world), plus "I have something specific in mind" and "You decide". This is the most important question. If they describe it, that description becomes the thesis's starting point.
2. **References.** "Is there a site, app or object you'd like it to feel like, and what do you like about it?" Separate appearance, behaviour and principle ([the eye](eye.md#using-references)). Never copy another brand's assets.
3. **The one thing.** "What should someone do or feel after ten seconds?" This decides the dominant element and the primary action.
4. **Who it is for.** "Who opens this, and in what situation?" Only if the request does not say.
5. **How loud.** "Should it be quiet and practical, or something people remember?" Offer the registers that fit the brief, in plain words, with examples of rich materials that would apply (photos, 3D, animation, ASCII, sound). Their answer sets the [register](register.md).
6. **Never.** "Anything you don't want? Colours, styles, things you've seen too often." Their exclusions are hard constraints.
7. **What they have.** "Do you have a logo, colours, fonts, photos or copy I should use?" Their assets come first, before anything sourced or generated.

A typical first round: 1, 3, 5 and 6, plus 7 when no brand exists in the repository.

## What to do with the answers

- **Write them in the record** under **Brief**, marked **user**. They outrank Kern's own decisions.
- **Their vision is the thesis's raw material.** Run the method as usual, but derive from what they described. Ground still matters: find the real facts that make their picture specific to them.
- **If what they want is on the median list, build it, and build it properly.** A person who wants a dark terminal-style site gets the best dark terminal-style site you can make: real material behind the theme, finished craft, honest content. The median rules exist to stop *defaults*, not to argue with someone's taste. You may raise one concern in one sentence, with a concrete way to make the choice stronger. Then do what they asked.
- **Fill the gaps with your own taste.** Everything they did not specify is still derived from the brief, the ground and the register. Say which parts were their decisions and which were yours.
- **Show your understanding before building** when the answers changed the direction: one or two sentences of thesis and register, so they can correct it cheaply. Do not wait for approval unless they asked to review first.

## Handing back

End with the thesis, which decisions came from them and which came from you, and what you departed from in the median. Ask whether it matches what they pictured, and offer the one or two changes most likely to bring it closer.
