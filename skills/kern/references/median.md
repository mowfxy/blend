# The median

The median is what models ship when nothing in the brief pushes them anywhere: the average of everything they have seen. It is competent, and that is exactly why it is dangerous. It looks finished, so nobody questions it, and because every model converges on it, products built with AI start to look like one product.

This file describes the median so you can recognise it in your own first instincts. **Nothing here is banned.** Any item can be right when the thesis arrives at it independently and you can say why in one sentence. The failure is reaching for it by reflex. In step 5 of the [method](method.md), write this brief's own median in the record, using this file as a prompt rather than a checklist.

The median moves. When a remedy becomes popular, models adopt it wholesale and it becomes the new median. "Warm editorial" was the cure for purple gradients until cream-and-terracotta with a serif headline became the most recognisable generated look of all. Treat anything that has become a fashion as suspect, including fashions that started as advice like this.

## Across all surfaces

- **Type:** Inter, Geist, SF or the system stack picked without considering alternatives. Two weights, 400 and 600. A serif headline with one italic word.
- **Colour:** violet or indigo accents. Purple-to-blue gradients. Near-black with one neon accent for anything technical or "AI". Beige and serif for anything "calm". Grey text on grey surfaces. One accent used everywhere until it means nothing.
- **Shape:** every element in a rounded rectangle with a soft shadow, all at the same radius, often 12 or 16. Glass panels with nothing behind them.
- **Icons:** one generic line set (Lucide, Heroicons, Feather, Tabler) at its default 2 px stroke, icons in tinted circles or squares, sparkles for anything AI, a rocket for launch, a shield for security, emoji standing in for icons.
- **Controls:** the component kit's defaults shipped as the design: a 40 px rounded-md button with a right arrow, a ghost secondary, a 1 px grey input with a blue focus ring, the same switch and the same toast on every product.
- **Words:** unlock, supercharge, elevate, seamless, effortless, powerful, all-in-one. "Not just X, it's Y." Three-word tricolons. "Get started" and "Learn more" on every button. Three of everything.
- **Motion:** fade-up on every block, hover-lift on every card, spring bounce on interface elements, a pulsing dot to look live.
- **Register:** everything at the same middle volume. A festival, a tax tool and a seed shop all get one hero, one accent and one fade, because nobody decided how loud each should be.

## Websites

- Centred hero with a pill eyebrow, headline, subhead, two buttons, and a screenshot in a tilted device frame.
- A logo strip, three icon-topped feature cards, a bento grid, testimonials, pricing with the middle plan highlighted, FAQ accordion, gradient CTA band and four-column footer.
- An animated background (particles, aurora blobs, glowing grid, glyph rain) behind a hero with nothing to show.
- A floating 3D blob or torus standing in for a missing product.
- The 2025–26 "technical" look: film grain on everything, mono labels in square brackets, ASCII or dithered decoration with no text-mode product behind it, a dot grid, a bento of tinted cards.
- The opposite failure, now just as common: a page for something people can see or taste with no pictures at all, grey type on off-white, and nothing that moves.
- Furniture text such as "Est. 2024", coordinates, local time, version numbers and "Scroll to explore".

## Web apps and SaaS

- A fixed left sidebar of 15 icons with labels, a top bar with search, a bell and an avatar, and a content area of cards.
- A settings page that is one long form with no grouping.
- Modals for everything, including flows that need context from the page behind them.
- Empty states as a grey icon above "No items yet".
- Toasts for errors that need the user's attention.

## Dashboards

- A row of four KPI tiles (number, label, green or red percentage with an arrow) above a line chart, above a table.
- Every chart in its own card, with the same height and the same title style.
- A donut chart for anything with parts.
- Rainbow categorical colours. Gridlines and legends at full strength.
- Metrics chosen because they were available, not because someone makes a decision with them.
- The time range hidden, or fixed to the last 30 days.

## Desktop apps

- **The default title bar,** grey or white, sitting above an app whose own surface is a completely different colour. The window controls live in a strip that belongs to the operating system rather than to the app. This is the most common desktop tell.
- A web page in a window: a website header inside the app, no native menus, no keyboard shortcuts, no window state memory, text selectable everywhere like a document, a hand cursor on every control.
- Electron or Tauri defaults: `titleBarStyle` untouched, no drag region, scrollbars styled for the web, no system accent colour, no respect for the system theme.
- Mobile patterns on desktop: hamburger menus, bottom sheets, giant touch-sized controls in a pointer-driven app.
- A sidebar that stops under the title bar instead of running full height beside the traffic lights.

## iOS and Android

- A web-style layout in a phone frame: hamburger menu, custom tab bar, centred titles everywhere, and custom back buttons that break the swipe-back gesture.
- Cards in a scroll view with drop shadows, ignoring the platform's grouped lists, sections and materials.
- A white status bar strip above a coloured app, which is the mobile version of the default title bar.
- Fixed font sizes that ignore Dynamic Type or text scaling.
- Onboarding as three swipeable illustrations before the app can be used.
- Custom alerts replacing system dialogs, sheets and menus.
- On Android, an iOS-style app shipped unchanged, with no edge-to-edge layout, no predictive back and no dynamic colour where it fits.

## Terminal

- Rainbow output, emoji status markers, ASCII-art banners on every run, spinners for operations that take 50 ms, and boxes drawn around everything. ASCII art itself is not the tell; a banner that delays every run is. A launch screen, an `--about` page or a TUI's idle view can carry real text-mode art.
- No `--json` and no quiet mode. Colour that ignores `NO_COLOR` or non-TTY output.

## Slides, email and documents

- A title slide with a gradient, then a bullet list per slide, three icons in a row, and "Thank you" at the end.
- Emails as tiny centred cards on a grey background, with the logo bigger than the message.
- Reports where every page has the same layout and every number has the same weight.

## Escaping the median

You do not escape the median by picking its opposite. That just gives you the anti-median, which is equally predictable. You escape it by going back to the ground: the facts, the material, the tension and the thesis. Let them produce a choice, then check the choice against this file. And do not escape it by going quiet: a page with every median element removed and nothing rich put back is the [avoidance aesthetic](eye.md#two-failure-modes), and it is converging just as fast. If it matches the median, ask whether the thesis really led there or your reflexes did.
